Policy

Privacy

Last updated: October 2, 2026

PhoneHome is a private, parent-controlled voice network for families. Your family’s calls, contacts, and voicemail live on a Mothership server — one you run yourself, or one run by someone you know and trust, like a family member or close friend. PhoneHome the company sells the license and relays iPhone ring notifications; it does not sell ads, sell data, or mine call content.

Three different places hold data, and this page covers each separately:

  • PhoneHome (the company) — billing, licensing, and push relay. No family contacts, call logs, voicemail, or audio.
  • Your Mothership (the host) — accounts, allowlists, call history, voicemail. Controlled by whoever runs it — ideally you, or someone you know and trust.
  • Your iPhone — your saved sign-in and app settings, plus whatever iOS itself keeps (like Recents).

What we never keep or track

Not by default, not in the fine print, not for “improving the product.”

  • No call recording Calls are never recorded. Only voicemails you choose to leave are saved.
  • No transcripts No cloud transcription or AI listening to calls.
  • No ads No advertising in the app, the website, or anywhere else.
  • No tracking or analytics No analytics, crash-reporting, or ad SDKs. No tracking cookies.
  • No selling or sharing Your family’s data is never sold, rented, or shared with marketers.
  • No location The app never asks for or reads where you are.
  • No address book The app never reads your iPhone contacts.
  • No family contacts at PhoneHome Circles and allowlists stay on your Mothership, not with us.
  • No call history at PhoneHome Who called whom, and when, stays on your Mothership.
  • No voicemail at PhoneHome Voicemails and greetings stay on your Mothership.
  • No passwords We never receive them, and they are never written to logs.
  • No card numbers Payments go straight to Stripe. We never see your card.

On a managed Mothership we run your family’s server for you, but we still never read, mine, or share what’s on it. Curious what we do keep? See section 1. Want to see how other kid phones compare? Privacy, compared.

1. What PhoneHome (the company) collects

PhoneHome is sold by the operator (“PhoneHome,” “we,” “us”). We collect only what we need to sell licenses and deliver iPhone calls:

  • Purchase and license records. When you buy a host license or a managed Mothership, we keep your billing email, Stripe customer and subscription IDs, your Mothership’s instance ID, the signed license and its expiry, plan limits, and any promo or referral code (plus referral credits). Card numbers go to Stripe and are never seen or stored by us.
  • Push relay. To make an iPhone ring, your Mothership sends our push gateway the phone’s Apple push token, a call ID, and the caller’s display name. We pass it to Apple and do not store it. Short operational logs record the Mothership instance ID, call ID, the last 8 characters of the push token, and whether delivery worked. The gateway never receives call audio, call setup (SDP), or encryption keys.
  • Support. If you email us, we keep that conversation. Support bundles from a managed Mothership are redacted of passwords and keys.
  • This website. No ads, analytics trackers, or tracking cookies. The optional email on the trial page is saved only in your own browser and is not sent to us. Pages are hosted on Cloudflare and downloads come from GitHub, which keep their own standard request logs.

What PhoneHome does not receive from a self-hosted Mothership:

  • Family member names (other than caller display names in a push), usernames, or passwords
  • Allowlists, Circles, Quiet Time, or other parent settings
  • Call history, voicemail, greetings, or any call audio

License checks happen on your Mothership against a signed key, offline. Your Mothership does not report usage back to us.

2. What your Mothership stores

Your Mothership stores the data it needs to run your family’s phone network. The person who runs it (the “operator”) is in charge of this data. That should be you or someone you know and trust — a parent, grandparent, or close family friend — because the operator’s server holds your family’s accounts, contacts, call history, and voicemail. Only join a Mothership whose operator you’d trust with that.

It stores:

  • Accounts: usernames, password hashes, display names, and parent/guardian links
  • Allowlists and Circles (who may call whom), Pods, and approved peer Motherships
  • Parent controls: Quiet Time, talk caps, Timeout, co-parent invites
  • Call history (who called whom, when, missed/answered) — not call audio
  • Voicemail and greetings that family members choose to record
  • iPhone push tokens, so calls can ring the right phone
  • Analog adapter (house phone) settings and credentials, if you use them

Who can see it depends on where it runs:

  • Self-hosted (Home Assistant add-on, Docker, your own PC): the data is on your hardware. PhoneHome has no access to it.
  • Managed Mothership: PhoneHome runs a separate, isolated server and database just for your family, on your behalf. We access it only to operate, back up, upgrade, or support it. Backups are encrypted before they leave that server. When you cancel, you can get an export, and we delete the server and its backups after the retention window.

3. What stays on your iPhone

The free iOS app keeps a small amount of data on the device itself:

  • Your username and password in the iOS Keychain (AfterFirstUnlockThisDeviceOnly — not synced to iCloud), so Face ID can sign you in. Signing out keeps it unless you choose Forget saved sign-in, delete your account, or delete the app.
  • The address of your Mothership and simple app preferences (like onboarding progress)
  • Microphone access, used only during calls and when you record voicemail or greetings

Some data is handled by Apple, not by PhoneHome:

  • Apple delivers ring and voicemail notifications using your phone’s push token.
  • PhoneHome calls appear in the iPhone Phone app’s Recents, like other calling apps. iOS may sync Recents to your other Apple devices depending on your iCloud settings.

The app has no advertising, analytics, or crash-reporting SDKs, and it does not read your location or your iPhone address book. Passwords are never written to logs.

Smaller targets: why there’s no central database

Big services keep every customer’s data in one place, so a single breach can expose millions of families at once. PhoneHome is built the other way around. Each Mothership holds only its own family circle — at most 50 phones — and PhoneHome the company keeps no shared database of families, contacts, or call history to leak.

  • Nothing pooled: your contacts and call history never sit next to strangers’ in one big database.
  • Little to steal from us: we hold license and billing records, not your family’s calls.
  • Managed Motherships stay separate too: each family gets its own isolated server and database, never a shared one.

No server is breach-proof. The operator should keep the Mothership updated, use a strong password, and reach it only over HTTPS. If something does go wrong, the damage stays inside one family circle instead of everyone’s.

Encryption

  • App ↔ Mothership: HTTPS
  • Softphone calls: WebRTC DTLS-SRTP
  • House phones (analog adapters): SIP TLS + SRTP — never cleartext public SIP
  • Mothership ↔ PhoneHome push gateway: HTTPS with a per-Mothership signed token

Your controls

  • Parents choose who kids can reach (mutual allowlist)
  • Deleting an account or kid line on the Mothership permanently erases its credentials, allowlists, call history, and voicemail
  • You can stop entirely by deleting accounts or shutting down your Mothership
  • To access or delete your purchase records with PhoneHome, email us at the address below

Children

PhoneHome is designed for family use under parental control. Guardians create kids’ lines and approve who is in their Circles. Kids’ data lives on the family’s Mothership, not with PhoneHome. This page is not legal advice; operators should review COPPA and local privacy law for their own setup.
