How PhoneHome compares

Kid “phones” and watches sell independence — then lock you into their device, their cloud, and a monthly plan. PhoneHome is the opposite: one small yearly license, a Mothership run by you or someone you trust, and any phone that still rings.

The short version: only the person who hosts the Mothership pays — $25/year, covering up to 50 phones across as many families as you like. Everyone who joins is free. It works with any phone you already own and keeps calls on a server you or someone you trust runs. Tin Can and kid watches charge every family per device, need their own hardware, and run calls through their cloud. No ads, no tracking, no location — see how our privacy stacks up.

Where your family’s calls live

The biggest difference isn’t price — it’s who holds your kids’ contacts and call history.

PhoneHome

On your Mothership

A server run by you or someone you know and trust. Every call is encrypted, app and house phone alike. PhoneHome the company never keeps call history, contacts, or voicemail — see exactly what we never keep.

Tin Can

On Tin Can’s servers

The company runs the network, so your family’s calling goes through its cloud.

Pinwheel

On Pinwheel’s servers

Call history, texts, photos, and GPS location sync to the Caregiver Portal, kept for 12 months.

Ooma

On Ooma’s network

A real phone line over the internet. Your call logs and voicemail sit with Ooma.

Kid watches & locked phones

On the carrier’s and brand’s servers

Calls, contacts, and the GPS location these devices are built to share.

Smaller target, smaller leak

When one company keeps millions of families’ contacts, call logs, and locations in a single cloud, one breach can expose all of them at once — which is exactly why big central databases are what attackers go after. PhoneHome is decentralized: each Mothership holds only its own circle, at most 50 phones, and there’s no PhoneHome database of families to steal because we never build one.

To be straight with you: no server is breach-proof, and a Mothership still needs care — keep it updated and reachable only over HTTPS. But if one is ever compromised, it’s one family circle, not everyone’s kids at once. How we limit what any server holds.

Privacy, compared — in their own words

We read each company’s published privacy policy. Here’s what they say they collect, next to ours.

PhoneHome

  • Call history: on your Mothership only — PhoneHome never gets it
  • Location: never collected — the app doesn’t even ask
  • Ads & analytics: none — no ads, analytics SDKs, or tracking cookies
  • Voicemail: on your Mothership

Tin Can

  • Call history: Tin Can keeps numbers, times, and call lengths; usage kept up to 12 months
  • Location: not a GPS device
  • Ads & analytics: cookies, pixels, and SDKs; targeted ads on other sites
  • Voicemail: recorded and stored by Tin Can

Pinwheel

  • Call history: collects call history, texts, and images; kept 12 months
  • Location: GPS location history, kept 12 months
  • Ads & analytics: kids’ data not used for ads; caregiver data shared with Meta, Google, and Klaviyo
  • Voicemail: not spelled out in its policy

Ooma

  • Call history: Ooma keeps your call logs and usage
  • Location: no GPS; approximate location from your IP, plus your 911 address
  • Ads & analytics: targeted ads and ad cookies; may count as a “sale” under state law
  • Voicemail: held on Ooma’s service

Gabb

  • Call history: with Gabb and its service partners
  • Location: collects precise device location
  • Ads & analytics: advertising cookies and ad networks on its sites
  • Voicemail: not spelled out in its policy

TickTalk

  • Call history: call content stays on the watch; account and device data with TickTalk
  • Location: always on — “you may not disable” it
  • Ads & analytics: no ads to kids; Google Analytics cookies
  • Voicemail: voice messages on the watch and parent app

Verizon Gizmo

  • Call history: Verizon collects call, messaging, and app usage
  • Location: precise location, kept up to 60 days
  • Ads & analytics: device IDs shared with Google Analytics
  • Voicemail: Verizon collects audio messages

Privacy-friendly Mixed Held or tracked by the company

To be fair: Tin Can says it doesn’t record calls, TickTalk says it never sells children’s data, Gabb says it doesn’t sell kids’ data either, Pinwheel says it never uses children’s data for ads, and Ooma won’t sell your calling records outside the company. These are decent companies. The difference is structural — with PhoneHome there’s no company cloud holding your kids’ calls in the first place.

What PhoneHome isn’t — and who to pick instead

At the end of the day, we want your kid to have the right tool, even if it isn’t ours. If one of these is a dealbreaker, the alternative we name is a good choice. We mean it.

It isn’t a phone your kid carries

No cellular service and no GPS. PhoneHome is the house phone, plus the app for family.

Pick instead: Pinwheel for a managed kids’ smartphone, or a watch like Gabb, TickTalk, or Verizon Gizmo.

It doesn’t call regular numbers or 911

It’s a private network for your family and friends. Kids can only reach people you approve.

Pick instead: Ooma for a real home line with 911 (about $130 once, then ~$6–$9/mo in taxes and fees), or Tin Can with Party Line ($9.99/mo). Either way, keep a real 911 path in the house.

It isn’t a tracker or monitoring tool

No location, no call recordings, no reading over shoulders. That’s on purpose, and it won’t change.

Pick instead: a GPS watch if you need location — and read how long they keep it.

It isn’t a big company

No store, no phone hotline, no 24/7 desk. Support is email, answered by the people who build it.

Pick instead: a carrier product like Verizon Gizmo if you want a store to walk into.

Frequently asked questions

How much does PhoneHome cost vs Tin Can?

Only the Mothership host pays: $25/year after a 30-day trial, covering up to 50 phones across any number of families. Families who join pay nothing. The app is free; a house phone usually needs a ~$40 HT801 plus a thrift or vintage RJ11 set. Tin Can is about $135 per Can plus Party Line at $9.99/mo to reach regular numbers, and every family buys its own.

Do grandparents, cousins, or friends have to pay?

No. If they join your Mothership, they’re covered by the host’s license — the app is free and there’s nothing to subscribe to. Their only possible cost is a house phone adapter, if they want one.

Can I use cool old / thrift analog phones?

Yes. Any RJ11 handset works through an adapter — Princess phones, Bakelite, novelty kids sets, cordless bases. The app needs no adapter.

How is this different from Gabb, TickTalk, or Gizmo?

Those are LTE watches and phones with monthly plans and GPS tracking — great on the go. PhoneHome is home calling for the family, on your own server, at a fraction of the ongoing cost.

How is this different from Pinwheel or Ooma?

Pinwheel is a managed kids’ smartphone: great controls, but it’s a screen in their pocket, with GPS history and a monthly plan per kid. Ooma is a real internet home phone with regular numbers and 911, but no kid-approved contact list, and every home pays its own fees. PhoneHome is screen-free house phones for the whole family circle, on a server you trust, for one $25/year license.

Can cousins and friends on other Motherships call us?

Yes. Friends can host their own Mothership, and calls between hubs work once the operators on both sides complete a peer invite.

Is PhoneHome safer from data breaches?

It’s a much smaller target. Kid-phone and calling companies keep every customer’s data in one cloud, so one breach can expose millions of families. PhoneHome has no central database of families — each Mothership holds only its own circle (up to 50 phones), and calls are encrypted. No server is breach-proof, so keep your Mothership updated; but a problem there affects one circle, not everyone. Why decentralized helps.

Does PhoneHome replace 911?

No. PhoneHome is a private family network. Keep a real emergency path — a mobile phone or landline — in the house.
